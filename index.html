Why the underlay matters

On a playground, the surface decides how hard a fall lands. SafeMark puts a resilient foam layer under the turf for exactly that job, and the sand infill is part of the system.

On roofs and patios, the pad adds give underfoot over concrete, tile or decking. Check drainage and roof load with your building engineer first.

Cushioning is about impact only. It says nothing about what the materials are made of. See materials

Installation, step by step

How SafeMark goes in

  1. A compacted base, prepared like a paver base, at a depth that lets the finished surface sit flush. A heavy-duty pressure-treated wood frame goes around the edge where there isn’t one.

  2. ShockPad sheets, 40 × 60 in and 0.75 in or 1 in thick, laid inside the frame.

  3. The sheets aren’t seamed together. Water drains down through the gaps between them.

  4. Laid perpendicular to the first layer. The two layers aren’t glued to each other.

  5. Pad edges are glued to the frame with BondMark. Nails are never used in a shock-pad installation.

  6. The turf is pulled taut, spot-glued to the pad about every 6 in, and glued to the frame along the edges.

  7. 3 lb of 20/40 silica sand per sq ft. Infill is required whenever turf goes over a shock pad.

The PFAS terms, and why they aren’t interchangeable

These phrases sound alike, but each one says something different about a product:

No intentionally added PFASA statement about the recipe. It doesn’t rule out traces from processing.
Not detectedThe specific PFAS a lab tested for weren’t found above its detection limit. It only means something if you know the method and the limit.
Targeted PFAS testingLooks for a set list of PFAS compounds, often a few dozen. It can miss fluoropolymers.
Total fluorine screeningA broader check for fluorine of any kind. It can’t name specific chemicals, but a low result is a stronger assurance.

When you review any product documentation, check which of these it describes.

Beyond PFAS: heat, wear and end of life

PFAS is one group of chemicals. Surface heat, wear and end-of-life planning are separate questions, and each is covered below.

How hot can the surface get?

Hotter than natural grass in direct sun, often by a lot. Most published measurements come from sports fields with rubber infill, not from residential landscape turf. Even so, they’re a fair warning: on sunny summer days, researchers have recorded synthetic surfaces from about 117°F to over 150°F while nearby grass stayed far cooler.

  • It cools quickly after sunset or in shade.
  • Spraying it with water cools it briefly, but the heat comes back.
  • Plan shade where children and pets play, and touch the surface before use on hot afternoons.
  • Reflected sun from windows can melt fibers. GardenMark’s warranty excludes this.

Sources: Penn State Center for Sports Surface Research (2012); New York State Department of Health synthetic turf fact sheet; NJ DEP memo, June 2022.

Does my installation need infill?

It depends on what’s underneath:

  • Montana, Kentucky and Canine on a crushed-stone base: no infill needed.
  • Augusta putting greens: infill is optional.
  • Any turf over a SafeMark shock pad: infill is required. That’s 3 lb of 20/40 silica sand per sq ft.

GardenMark’s installation guides don’t call for crumb-rubber infill. If your installer suggests infill, ask exactly what it is.

Landscape turf isn’t sports turf

Much of the public research and debate, including EPA’s tire crumb work, concerns athletic fields filled with recycled tire rubber. GardenMark’s landscape installations don’t use tire crumb. That’s a real difference, but it doesn’t make the questions about heat, plastics and disposal go away.

Drainage, cleaning and care

Drainage depends on the base: 3–4 in of ¾ in crushed stone topped with 1–2 in of stone dust or concrete sand, compacted and given a slight slope.

  • Brush the pile up with a stiff rake or power brush, especially in busy spots.
  • Clear leaves and debris so they don’t break down on the surface.
  • Rinse pet areas often. Ask your dealer before using any cleaning product.
  • Matting in high-traffic areas is normal wear, and the warranty doesn’t cover it.

The warranty, in plain English.

A 20-year limited warranty on GardenMark grass, prorated. It’s a promise about the material. It isn’t a promise that the lawn will look new for 20 years.

  • What it covers: grass that substantially changes from UV degradation, beyond a change in texture. GardenMark supplies replacement grass.
  • What you pay: labor to remove and reinstall, plus a prorated share of the replacement grass as the years go by.
  • The limit: GardenMark’s liability can’t exceed the original invoice price of the grass.
  • What it excludes: installation damage, improper underlay, matting and normal wear, chemicals and improper cleaning, animals, reflected-sun melting, natural disasters, and installs not done by a professional installer.
  • To activate it: email the completed form, your original invoice and photos of the installation to warranty@garden-mark.com, and keep the confirmation email GardenMark sends back.

    Questions people actually ask.

    Will it look artificial?

    That comes down to the product, the light and the installation. Montana and Kentucky blend three greens over a thatch layer to avoid a flat, uniform color. Clean edges, consistent pile direction and real planting around the turf matter just as much. See samples outdoors before you decide.

    Is GardenMark grass PFAS-free?

    GardenMark identifies its synthetic grass as PFAS-free. Contact us for available product documentation. PFAS terms explained

    How hot can the surface get?

    In direct summer sun, much hotter than grass. Studies of synthetic fields have measured roughly 117–150°F and more. Plan for shade and check the surface before children or pets play on it. More on heat

    What maintenance remains?

    Brushing the pile up, clearing leaves and debris, rinsing (more often for pets), and fixing seams or edges if they lift. It takes far less time than a lawn, but it isn’t zero.

    How do pet cleaning and drainage work?

    Liquids drain through the turf backing into the crushed-stone base, so drainage depends on how well that base is built. Remove solid waste and rinse wet spots regularly. Canine is made with antimicrobial additives. Check with your dealer before using any cleaner.

    What does the warranty cover?

    It covers replacement grass, prorated, if the grass substantially degrades from UV within 20 years. It doesn’t cover labor, normal wear, matting or damage. A professional installation and registration are required. Plain-English summary

    Does my application require infill?

    Not for Montana, Kentucky or Canine over a standard stone base. It’s optional for Augusta, and required (silica sand) over a SafeMark shock pad. Infill details

    Can I install it myself?

    GardenMark publishes detailed guides, but its warranty doesn’t apply when the grass isn’t installed by a professional installer.
