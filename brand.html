A careful refresh of the GardenMark logo

Same name, same leaf-tile idea, same greens. It’s cleaner to draw, easier to read at small sizes, and works in one color.

What changed and why

  • Symbol. The loose cluster of rounded squares becomes three leaf tiles on a strict two-by-two grid. Each tile has two softened opposite corners, taken from the shape of the existing icon. The empty fourth cell keeps the open, growing feel of the original and gives the mark a silhouette you can recognize at 16 px.
  • Wordmark. The rounded slab with a leaf inside the G is replaced by Host Grotesk Semibold with tightened spacing. The camel-case “GardenMark” and the ® stay. The leaf now lives in the symbol only, so the two parts no longer compete.
  • Descriptor. “Artificial Grass” moves out of the primary lockup. It’s still available as a secondary lockup for packaging, roll labels and trade-show material, where the product category isn’t obvious.
  • Color. The two existing greens stay. The lime is reserved for the lighter tile and for accents on dark backgrounds, so it never carries small text on white.

This is a proposal. The original artwork wasn’t available as vector files. Before production, check the refinement against GardenMark’s trademark registration and redraw it from the original masters.
