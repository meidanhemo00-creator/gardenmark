Research, positioning and claims register

Internal working document for the GardenMark website concept. Research conducted October 16, 2026. Not for publication.

Evidence labels used throughout: Verified read directly in a primary or company source; Observation something we noticed or a secondary report we couldn’t confirm; Hypothesis informed judgment, not measured; Do not use unsupported, outdated or contradicted.

1. Strategic direction in brief

Position GardenMark as the synthetic grass you can actually check. The category is crowded with “PFAS-free”, “cooler” and “eco” badges that rarely show their working. GardenMark has a smaller claim set but real documents: published specs, recent installation guides and a public 20-year limited warranty. The site turns that into a trust advantage. It says what’s documented, what isn’t, and what to ask.

Lead with desire, then earn trust. The emotional promise is pride in a usable, good-looking outdoor space, carried by family and pet lifestyle imagery that is clearly labeled as illustrative. The proof is GardenMark’s own installations (urban townhouse yards, shore houses, rooftops) and an honest close-up of the material.

Speak Northeast first. GardenMark is a Northern New Jersey supplier whose real gallery is mostly NJ/NY urban and shore projects. In this climate, “saves water” is a weak and partly misleading lead. The honest leads are no mowing, no mud in the turfed area, green in shade and high-traffic spots, and a simpler routine.

Conversion: “Find a dealer” is the primary action, because GardenMark sells through dealers and its warranty requires professional installation. “Request a sample” is not offered, because no sample program could be confirmed.

Conflicts and stale content on the current site

  • Warranty: 20 years (warranty page and 2026 PDF) vs “fifteen year warranty” (FAQ). The homepage badge image is named “15-warranty” but labeled 20 years.
  • Infill: product pages say “Infill not needed”. The Augusta guide says it’s optional. The shock-pad guide requires sand. The FAQ says GardenMark “uses Durafill” coated infill.
  • SafeMark: product pages call it SafeMark, while the spec text calls the same system “GreenPlay”. The site says the pad is recycled PE foam, but the 2025 spec says “100% cross link foam PE + PU adhesive” and doesn’t mention recycled content.
  • Canine: “stays cool to the touch” is contradicted by independent heat data and has no test behind it.
  • All four turf data-sheet links (garden-mark.com/…/2022/11 and 2023/01) returned 404 on Oct 16, 2026.
  • BondMark coverage: “15 to 20 feet” per bottle (accessories page) vs “one tube per 25 linear feet” (2026 guide).
  • Template leftovers are live: lorem ipsum testimonials (“Michale John”), “Hire Us For Any Service & Get 25% Off”, “24/7 availability”, and a “24 hour emergency number 1800 856 7489” on the installer page.
  • LEED credit codes in the FAQ (SS61, WE1, MR4…) refer to an old LEED version.

7. Imagery policy

  • Every product and project claim uses GardenMark’s own photos, tagged “GardenMark project photo” or “Product photo supplied by GardenMark”. Locations and products used aren’t stated, because GardenMark’s gallery doesn’t record them.
  • Every photograph on the site is a GardenMark photo that shows its synthetic grass. All stock lifestyle photography was removed in the third round. People and pets will come from AI lifestyle images briefed in Image briefs, labeled as illustrative.
  • The hero and product panels use rendered sample illustrations (src/sample.js), drawn in true perspective from documented attributes only: pile height, three greens and thatch color, plus a regulation 1.68 in golf ball for Augusta. Each is labeled as an illustration and paired with GardenMark’s real product photo. The SafeMark exploded view and plan view are labeled conceptual diagrams built from the spec sheet and installation guide.
  • No before/after slider: there are no matched pairs. No testimonials, awards or customer names.
  • Excluded from the gallery: images showing third-party trademarks (NFL logo, ASICS event).
  • The source photos are about 960 px wide and look heavily saturated. They’re upscaled 1.6× for retina without altering color. New photography is the biggest single upgrade available.

10. What was tested

  • Desktop at 1440×900 and mobile at 390×844 (Playwright Chromium), plus the in-app browser pane.
  • Scroll sequence: each stage checked at progress 0, 0.25, 0.45, 0.68 and 0.9. Static fallback checked with reduced motion and on short viewports.
  • Space tabs and product tabs by click and keyboard (arrow keys, Home/End); product chips select the right style.
  • Warranty schedule toggle; gallery filters; lightbox by click, arrow keys and Escape.
  • Cost tool: break-even, no break-even within the period, never breaks even, and empty states.
  • Dealer form: empty submit errors, invalid ZIP, valid flow, mailto contents, edit and copy.
  • Horizontal overflow at 390 px, console errors, broken image requests, and status codes of all external PDF links.
